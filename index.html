Learning
Blue is what you can read — every word the level allows you. Green is what you can use: the same measure over the words you have actually written yourself. Green always trails blue, and the gap between them is normal — recognising a word is easier than reaching for it, and that gap widens as you improve. An estimate of how much of the next level you can already read, not how much of its list you have ticked off — the commonest words carry most of the text, so the first few hundred matter far more than the rest. 95% is where reading stops being work; published research puts adequate comprehension there and comfortable independent reading at 98%. Words you have written yourself count double, since using a word is harder than recognising it.
Graded readers introduce roughly one new word per 40–50 characters of text you already know. The partner is offered the three commonest words you have not met and uses one if it fits naturally; if none fit, the credit carries to the next turn. A new word stays highlighted and gets reused until you have seen it three times.
Switches the allowlist, the starters and the prompt. Words you added keep the form you added them in. About 3% of entries list several traditional variants (岸/㟁, 幫/幇/幚); the one belonging to the word's main reading is used.

Words by level

Grouped by what you have done with each word, commonest first inside each group — which is the order they are worth learning in. Only Not yet and Ticked as already known have boxes, because those are the two you can change: an empty box means the word is not usable yet, and ticking it says you already know it, which joins it to the allowlist at once. Words at your level or already met need no box — they are usable either way. Ticked words stay out of 词 and the flashcard exports on purpose: that list is for words you brought with you, not ones the app taught you.

English explanation

This chat is not restricted to your word list in either direction — ask anything, in Chinese or English, and answers may use any words needed to explain clearly.

Added vocabulary

On the phone these open the share sheet — send the file to Pleco, or to Files and import it in Anki. Pleco: Flashcards → Import Cards. Anki desktop: File → Import.
